02 — proof

Selected work

Every write-up answers three questions: what was there before, what I changed, and what was different afterwards. The stripe on the left shows which line the work belongs to.

Delivery

Cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 6

Platform team · 2024 · 4 engineers

GitHub ActionsArgo CDHelmTerraform
Before
Releases were run by hand from a wiki checklist. A typical deploy took 40 minutes, needed a senior engineer on standby, and failed about once in five attempts.
What I changed
Rebuilt the pipeline as a single GitHub Actions workflow: build, test, image push, then a GitOps sync with Argo CD. Environment config in Terraform, staging and production sharing one module. A manual approval gate only for production.
After
Median deploy time 6 minutes.40 min → 6 min deployfailures ≈ 1 in 40

Platform

Moved 40 services onto a shared Kubernetes platform

Cloud engineering · 2023–2024

EKSTerraformHelmIstioExternalDNS
Before
Each team ran its own EKS cluster — 40 services across 9 clusters, no shared ingress, no consistent autoscaling, cloud spend growing faster than traffic.
What I changed
Designed a single multi-tenant EKS platform: namespaces per team, one Istio ingress, consistent Helm chart templates and a Terraform module every team used to onboard a service.
After
Two clusters instead of nine. Onboarding went from weeks to hours.9 clusters → 2onboarding 2 wks → 1 day

Reliability

SLO-based alerting that cut pages by 70%

SRE · 2024

PrometheusGrafanaAlertmanagerOpenTelemetry
Before
Alerts fired on host metrics — CPU, disk, memory. On-call was paged dozens of times a week and most pages were noise. Real incidents were usually found by users first.
What I changed
Defined SLIs and SLOs for the top user journeys, moved alerting to burn-rate alerts on those SLOs, added OpenTelemetry tracing, and rewired runbooks so every alert links to one.
After
Pages down ~70%. Detection in minutes, not a quarter hour.pages −70%MTTD 15 → ≺ 3 min
